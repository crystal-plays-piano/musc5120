# Renaissance Music

## Overview

- Dates: Approx 1400 -> 1600 AD

- Renaissance means "Rebirth"
    - if something is to be "re*born*", then it first has to die

- Term is more appropriate for visual arts / literature
    - rediscovery of Ancient Greek and Roman arts (Classical / Antiquity)
        - problem: we don't have very much music from Antiquity
        - nothing to *go back* to
    - first use of term "Renaissance" to describe this era in the 1850s
        - during birth / explosion of Musicology

## Humanism

- emphasis on knowledge (+ learning) for "good moral character"

- definirely relates to Ancient Greece
    - Humanism

- new ideas of a "cultivated aristocrat"

- Humanism is tied to a growth of education
    - still more restricted by class, sex, race, but more than just "in the church"
    - education involves:
        - grammar
        - logic
        - rhetoric

- focus on music study as a discipline related to Humanism changes music from science -> humanities
    - new idea of *music as an expressive language*
        - communicating emotions and thoughts: music has *meaning*

## Social History of Renaissance Era

- **strong** patronage system
    - patrons are subject to monarch, but +/- independent : "self-sustaining"
    - headed by POWERFUL families
        - Medici Family!!
    - both secular AND sacred functions
    - performance >>>>>>>>>> composition
        - we don't need to write music we need to play/hear music

### Life of a Renaissance Musician

- musicians employed by the church aren't always devoted to the church
- skills are passed down by families
- music is leisure for the aristocracy

- musicians were "craftsmen", not "artists"
- more amateurs
    - Aristocrats don't need the money, so they don't have to be good
- increasing internationalism
    - musicians move between courts, churches, patrons: musicians *MOVE*

- instruments belong to patrons, not musicians
    - instrumentalists play families, but not specific instruments

- music is a BUSINESS
    - people would be RECRUITED to work as musicians

## Johannes Tinctoris (1436 - 1511)

- music theorist and scholar - LEADING THEORIST AND SCHOLAR

- wrote 13 treatises, primarily music theory

- wrote first dictionary of musical terms\*
    - \* first that we know

### 1477 Tinctoris Treatise

- Nothing over 40 years old is worth performing or hearing
- "The horizons of music have been so wonderfully extended that it seems a new art"
    - best examples come *from England*
        - moved from France -> England

### John Dunstable (ca. 1390 - 1453)

- Sometimes also named John Dunstaple

- most of career in France
    - stylistically English, geographically France
    - *English Occupation during Hundred Year's War*

- primarily writes sacred music
    - best known for motets (25% of his music)

## Renaissance Era Polyphony

- vs Medieval Polyphony
    - Emphasis on consonance, including imperfect consonance
    - Carefully controlled dissonance
    - Avoiding parallel P5 and P8
        - redefinition of Consonance and Dissonance
        - Consonance and Dissonance are social constructs
    - broader range
    - stratified voices (less voice crossing)
